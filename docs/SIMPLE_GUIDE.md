# YewNo Release: The Simple Guide

**No programming knowledge needed.**

## What is this?

Think about how scattered your digital life is. Your emails are in Gmail. Your texts are on your phone. Photos are split between your phone and laptop. Your fridge, stove, and dryer each have their own app. Your WiFi has another app. Bills come from five different companies. Your bank has its own login.

**YewNo Release puts all of it in one place, with one search bar.**

Type "fridge", see your fridge alerts. Type "bill", see every bill from every company. Type "flight", there's your confirmation email. One box. Everything you own.

## Is it safe?

Yes, this was the top priority. In plain English:

- **Your money stuff is locked.** Banking, bills, and account info are scrambled (encrypted) before being stored. Even if someone stole the database file, they'd see unreadable gibberish.
- **Your password is never stored.** Only a scrambled fingerprint of it is. Nobody, not even the app, can read your actual password back.
- **It lives on YOUR computer.** Nothing is sent to a company's cloud. You own the data and the machine it sits on.
- **Everything is on the record.** Every sign in and search is logged, so you can always see if anything unusual happened.
- **It blocks guessing attacks.** If someone tries passwords over and over, the app automatically slows them down and shuts them out.

## How do I use it?

1. **Open the app** in your web browser.
2. **Create an account**: pick a username and a password of at least 10 characters.
3. **Search.** Type anything in the big box and press Enter.
4. **Filter if you want.** Click a pill under the search bar, "gmail", "iot" (that's your smart home devices), "banking", to only search that one source. Click "all" to search everything again.
5. **Look for the lock.** Results with a 🔒 came from your protected sources (banking, bills, accounts) and are stored encrypted.

That's the whole app. One search bar, some filter pills, your results.

## Questions people ask

**Where does my data come from?**
Small helper programs called *connectors*, one watches your Gmail, one talks to your smart fridge, one reads your bank feed, and they each drop what they find into your one central index. A technical person sets these up once; after that it's automatic.

**Can other people see my stuff?**
No. Every account only ever sees its own data. Even on a shared install, searches are walled off per person.

**What if there are no results?**
The app can pass your search to the live internet instead, so an empty result still gets you an answer.

**What do businesses use this for?**
Same problem, bigger scale: a company's customer records, support tickets, emails, and billing all live in different systems that don't talk. YewNo Release gives the whole company one search bar over all of it, hosted on their own servers, so nothing leaves the building.

## If something goes wrong

- **"Rate limit exceeded"**: you (or something) searched too fast. Wait ten seconds.
- **"Session expired"**: you were signed in over 8 hours. Just sign in again. This is a safety feature.
- **"Invalid username or password"**: exactly what it says; passwords are case sensitive.
- **Password rules**: at least 10 characters. A short sentence you'll remember ("purple dryer sings at 3pm") beats a complicated short one.
